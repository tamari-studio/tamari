# How we write

> A starting point. Every line below is a guess at how a small team writes articles and
> newsletters. Change each one until it is true for you, and delete what you don't do. Part of
> [[07-Maps/Team]].

## From notes to published
1. **Notes first.** Collect what you know before you write a sentence: a voice memo transcript,
   bullets, a rough draft. Facts, names and numbers go in as they are.
2. **Pick the voice.** Open the Writer space in Tamari and choose the style guide that fits the
   place the piece will appear. One piece, one voice.
3. **Write the draft.** Paste the notes and select **Write draft**. It takes up to 45 seconds.
   Without an AI key, type the draft yourself on the same page. Everything else works the same.
4. **Fill the gaps.** The draft marks every missing fact as `[NEEDS: ...]`. Nothing is invented for
   you. Fill each gap from the notes or the source, or cut the sentence. A piece with a gap left
   does not go to a reader.
5. **One reader.** Someone else reads it once, for facts and tone. They change nothing without
   telling the author.
6. **Mark it final.** Select **Mark as final** on the draft in Tamari, then publish. Copy the text
   from there so the published version and the final one are the same.

## What finished means
- Every name, number and date has been checked against its source.
- Every quote was said by that person and they know it will be printed.
- The headline says what the piece is about, in under 70 characters.
- Nothing in it needs a second explanation.

## Who owns a piece
- Every piece has one author, who keeps its draft in Tamari current and answers for its facts.
- The author picks the reader. The reader is never the person who gave the author the notes.

## Our voices
- **Plain and warm:** for customers. Short sentences, "we" and "you", one concrete example first.
- **Crisp newsletter:** for the Friday mail. Dry, quick, one joke at most.
- Add a voice as a style guide in Tamari when a new place needs one. Paste in three of the best
  pieces written there, so the guide shows the voice instead of describing it.
